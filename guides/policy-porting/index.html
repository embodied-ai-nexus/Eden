    Porting a Pretrained Policy¶

    A frozen policy trained in another stack (IsaacGym, Isaac Lab, mjlab, MuJoCo, rl_games, an exported ONNX graph) runs in Eden for inference and sim-to-sim as a policy extension, not as a task. The policy keeps its weights and its observation and action contract; Eden supplies the robot, the scene and the control loop. This page is the checklist for reproducing such a policy faithfully, and the order in which to verify it. How to package the result (the class, its files, the registry) is in Adding a Policy to the Zoo.

    Training a new policy is a different job

    This page is for reproducing a policy someone else trained. To train in Eden, write a task config instead: see Task Configuration.

    The ports that ship with Eden are worked examples, all under eden/extensions/policies/:

    • husky (G1 skateboarding, ONNX) declares its observation and action through the training task's own terms: the model to follow for a new port.
    • holosoma, homie, sonic assemble their input from raw simulator state, which is what lets them ride along as extra slots in a deployment.
    • twist2, omnixtreme, humanoid_gpt (ONNX motion trackers), protomotionv3 (a torch actor loaded from a training checkpoint), and simtoolreal (a 29-DOF arm and hand with an rl_games LSTM actor reimplemented in torch).

    The Policy Zoo lists every one with what it needs.

    The shape of a port¶

    A port is one PolicyBase subclass whose classmethods describe the world the policy was trained in:

    Member What to put there
    robot_options() The robot, as an eden.robots options object (G1Dofs29(...), KukaSharpa(...)) with the source's default pose, gains and armature.
    scene_entity_options() Every other entity the policy reads: the object it manipulates, the board it rides, a goal marker, or a plane entity that replaces the flat ground (for example with terrain).
    env_options() sim_dt, decimation and solver settings: the control rate and physics the policy expects.
    action_options() The action terms its output drives.
    observation_options() The observation groups it reads.
    command_options() The command terms it consumes, if any (a velocity command, a motion reference).
    step(obs) One inference call, returning {action_term_name: tensor}.
    reset(envs_idx) Clear recurrent state and per-episode buffers.

    robot_options() and scene_entity_options() together are the scene: eden inference --policy <name> without a --task builds an empty scene from exactly those two. A policy whose observation is defined against something other than its robot (SimToolReal's tool on its table, HUSKY's skateboard) must declare that entity, or a task-less run spawns a lone robot and the policy fails resolving the entity. Resolve each entity with self.require_entity(name) in __init__ so a scene that lacks it fails at construction, and keep reading it through self.env.entities[name] in step(), because deployment swaps the entity for a live-robot stand-in.

    Declare the observation; don't assemble it in step()¶

    A policy that owns its env (the task-less eden inference --policy case) should state its input as observation_options(), with command_options() for any command or clock it observes and action_options() scaled so that last_action is the actor's raw output. It then sets reads_env_observations = True, and its step() just feeds obs[<group>] to the network. The layout is explicit and reusable, and it gets the observation manager's history, scale, NaN and reset handling for free.

    husky is the worked example. Its observation_options() and action_options() reuse the training task's actor group and action term (eden/tasks/husky/config.py), so the released checkpoint and a policy trained in Eden share one layout, and a held ConstantSkateCommand carries the command and the phase clock. tests/extensions/policies/husky/test_policy.py pins that env against a hand-written formula of the source's observation.

    A command term that ticks must tick in pre_physics_advance

    A deployment never runs post_physics_refresh, so a command term a frozen policy observes (a phase clock, a held command) must advance its state in a hook that runs on hardware too. HUSKY's ConstantSkateCommand ticks in pre_physics_advance for that reason.

    Assembling the input from raw sim state inside step() is for policies that have to ride along as extra slots on someone else's env: the Holosoma safety policy eden deploy inserts on a G1, and controllers stacked next to it. One env publishes one observation layout, so only one env-reading policy can be loaded at a time (see Deployment). Such a policy's observation_options() may return {}: no groups at all builds and steps fine, as holosoma does. What is not allowed is declaring a group and leaving it empty: a group with concatenate_terms=True and no terms fails at construction with Unable to concatenate observation terms in group `<name>`. Declare one real term or no group.

    The porting checklist¶

    Match the source exactly, in this order. Each step's failure is invisible to the steps before it, which is why the order matters.

    1. The I/O contract¶

    Reproduce the observation layout and order, any input normalization, and the action transform bit for bit.

    • Index every DOF and link by name. Genesis' native DOF order differs from the source's. Resolve the names once with robot.find_named_dofs(POLICY_JOINT_NAMES), then hand its .columns to tensor reads (default_dofs_*, full-width get_dofs_*()) and its .dofs_idx_local to Genesis calls (dofs_idx_local=). Never index a default_dofs_* tensor with local indices: they are a different index space (see DOF ordering).
    • Check the built robot's frozen constants by name, in every env. robot.get_default_dofs_pos(POLICY_JOINT_NAMES) returns (num_envs, n) in the policy's order; compare it against the source's default pose at construction, as HuskyPolicy.__init__ does.
    • Quaternions are wxyz in Eden, while many sources put xyzw in their observation vector.
    • Mind world vs. base frame for every absolute term.
    • Read object and free-body poses with get_pos(relative=True) / get_quat(relative=True). The default returns Genesis' solver frame, re-originated at the center of mass, not the declared root pose the source reports (see Which frame?). This one fails quietly.

    2. The network¶

    Use OnnxModule from eden/extensions/policies/onnx.py for feedforward ONNX actors (twist2, sonic and humanoid_gpt wrap it). Reimplement exotic backends in plain torch, as simtoolreal/network.py does for an rl_games LSTM. Carry recurrent state across step() and clear it in reset(). When you load weights, confirm there are no missing or unexpected keys.

    3. The dynamics¶

    Match the robot's base pose, PD gains (default_dofs_kp / default_dofs_kd), armature (default_dofs_armature: Eden fills armature=0.1 on every URDF joint whose asset armature is zero, often far too high for a low-stiffness finger; see Armature defaults), joint friction, control rate (sim_dt × decimation) and gravity compensation. The objects and the scene must match the world the policy trained in.

    4. Verify, in layers¶

    This is what catches the bugs.

    1. Observation parity. Feed the same state (q, q̇, …) to the source's observation function and to your assembly, and diff field by field (expect differences around 1e-7). This validates the math given a state, so synthetic poses never reveal that you read the sim in the wrong frame: also diff a few sim-measured states against the source's convention.
    2. Action parity. Feed the same observation to the source's policy and to yours, and diff the action. A faithful network reimplementation is bit-identical.
    3. Dynamics audit. Dump the built robot's per-DOF kp, kd and armature (get_dofs_armature()), plus object mass, placement and control rate, and compare them with the source. Passing observation and action parity does not mean the rollout matches: mismatched dynamics make the trajectory diverge while both parity checks stay green. When a ported policy underperforms with parity passing, look here first.
    4. Sim-to-sim rollout. Only after the above, run the closed loop and render it. Two rungs: eden inference --policy <name> --async replays the loop in Genesis with deployment-style timing, and eden deploy --policy <name> --mujoco --menagerie replays it against MuJoCo, which catches dynamics assumptions only Genesis satisfies (see Sim-to-sim validation).

    Why the closed loop cannot be the first check

    A policy servos whatever pose it is shown, so a frame or gain mismatch still converges: it just converges to the wrong place. A constant offset between object and goal is the classic symptom of the relative= mistake. A rollout that "mostly works" is not evidence of a correct port.
